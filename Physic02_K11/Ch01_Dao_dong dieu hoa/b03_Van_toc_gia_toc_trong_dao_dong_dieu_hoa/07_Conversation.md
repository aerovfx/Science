
**Journalist:** Thank you for joining us today. People often think of physics as numbers, formulas, and experiments. But you call yourself a *philosopher of physics*. What does that really mean?

**Philosopher:** It means I’m less interested in just calculating outcomes, and more in asking: *What do those numbers tell us about reality?* Physics explains *how* the universe moves. Philosophy asks *why* it moves the way it does—and what that means for us.

**Journalist:** Let’s take a simple example: velocity and acceleration. To most people, those are just high school concepts. Why do you think they’re more profound?

**Philosopher:** Velocity is about direction and speed. It mirrors how we experience life—always moving somewhere, sometimes faster, sometimes slower. It’s relatable, almost human. Acceleration, however, is about change itself. And change is what unsettles us, thrills us, even overwhelms us. Philosophically, acceleration represents transformation—the very heartbeat of the universe.

**Journalist:** That’s fascinating. So when a student learns the formula for acceleration, you’re saying there’s more behind it?

**Philosopher:** Exactly. Behind every equation lies a metaphor. A falling apple doesn’t just tell us about gravity; it tells us that nature has order. The swing of a pendulum doesn’t just trace a curve; it whispers that balance and rhythm are woven into existence.

**Journalist:** Some people argue that physics strips the world of mystery by explaining it. Do you agree?

**Philosopher:** Quite the opposite. Explanation deepens mystery. When I learn *why* the stars move the way they do, I don’t feel less wonder—I feel more. Physics gives us clarity, but philosophy ensures we don’t lose the poetry of that clarity.

**Journalist:** Beautifully said. So, if you had one message for our audience about physics and philosophy, what would it be?

**Philosopher:** Never see science as cold or distant. Physics is the story of motion, and philosophy is the reflection on its meaning. Together, they remind us that we live in a universe that is not only intelligible—but also endlessly inspiring.
