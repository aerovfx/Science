Lesson 4: Harmonic Motion Exercises – Summary

1. Review of Basic Theory
	•	Harmonic motion equation:
x = A \cos(\omega t + \phi)
	•	A: amplitude
	•	\omega: angular frequency
	•	\phi: initial phase
	•	Relationship with uniform circular motion:
	•	Harmonic motion is the projection of uniform circular motion.
	•	Equilibrium position → center O, amplitude → radius, period → time for a full rotation.

⸻

2. Determining Quantities from the Equation
	•	Amplitude: A
	•	Angular frequency: \omega
	•	Period: T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}
	•	Frequency: f = \frac{1}{T}
	•	Initial phase: \phi
	•	Phase at time t: \phi(t) = \omega t + \phi

⸻

3. Examples and Solutions

Example 1:
x = 2 \cos(4\pi t - \pi/6)
	•	Amplitude: A = 2\text{ cm}
	•	Angular frequency: \omega = 4\pi \text{ rad/s}
	•	Period: T = 0.5 \text{ s}
	•	Frequency: f = 2 \text{ Hz}
	•	Initial phase: \phi = -\pi/6
	•	Phase at t = 1\text{ s}: \phi(t) = 23\pi/6

⸻

Example 2:
A particle oscillates along Ox with A = 10\text{ cm}, T = 2 \text{ s}. At t = 0, the particle is at the positive extreme.

a) Motion equation:
x = 10 \cos(\pi t)

b) Time when the particle first passes x = 5 \text{ cm}:
5 = 10 \cos(\pi t) \implies t = 1/3 \text{ s}

⸻

Example 3:
Based on the displacement–time graph:
	•	Amplitude: A = 15 \text{ cm}
	•	Period: T = 0.12 \text{ s}
	•	Frequency: f \approx 8.33 \text{ Hz}
	•	Angular frequency: \omega \approx 0.24\pi \text{ rad/s}
	•	Initial phase: \phi = -\pi/2 \text{ rad}
	•	Motion equation:
x = 15 \cos(0.24\pi t - \pi/2)

⸻

4. Conclusion

To solve harmonic motion exercises:
	1.	Identify the basic quantities: A, \omega, T, f, \phi
	2.	Use the equation or the graph to find the phase and the time the particle passes a specific position
	3.	The relationship with uniform circular motion can be used for calculations
